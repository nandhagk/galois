## Review and proof notes

Partition has an empty-token `gamma_top` conflict, and its shared carrier does not pin lattice operations. Its evaluation and collapse bounds omit changing-accumulator joins, and fresh tokens can increase widening rank. Disjunction needs a prepared bounded-rank carrier with initialization and preservation, a treatment of equivalent representatives and a concrete `JOIN_SIZE` provider. RelEnv needs a refinement work and endpoint-size budget, correspondence between shared product operations and concretization, and full `NUM_STATE_T` lattice time contracts. BitsChecks needs meanings and precision rules for other overflow operators and malformed arities, operand widths, and range work and endpoint-size budgets. Client compilation, seeds and queries lack numerical classes. `POINTER_LINK` has no concrete source provider, and Steensgaard needs exact source-congruence quotient reflection. Client tests wait until after batch 2 because Run code is written in this batch.

The F1 round 19 findings propose a shared disjunction join-size law. F1 round 21 withdraws `NUM_STATE_TIME.swf` and its preservation laws. `T_assume_le` has no invariant premise. The numeric layer will supply full `LATTICE_TIME` contracts, widening and narrowing bounds, and `range_size` for endpoint bits. RelEnv needs these raw-state contracts for its product and range refinement. F1-342 to F1-349 are being implemented. Check the released interfaces before proving the dependent contracts.

These are review questions, not new premises. Keep published statements unchanged. Report a public contract defect as described in section 5.

## 3. How to read a Sig and its plan
- FooSig.v has layers: FOO_OPS (code), FOO_SPEC (definitions), FOO_LAWS (laws),
  FOO_TIME (T_op, constants c_*, contracts), FOO_PLAN (internals, k_* constants, lemma chain).
- Foo.v has FooCode <: FOO_OPS, Foo <: FOO (Include FooCode), FooT <: FOO_ALL (Include Foo).
- Your targets are the Admitted lemmas in your files, tagged CONVERT, STUB, SIG-QUESTION, PLAN-DEFECT or OPEN.
- A PLAN lists lemmas F1..Fn (functional) then T1..Tn (time), ending at a contract.
  Prove them in order; each may use the earlier ones. You choose the values of k_* and c_*.
- A Parameter of another component's Sig may be used before it is proved. Your tests may
  run only code that exists in this base.
- Time functions, callback companions, `c_*`, `k_*` and time-bound potentials have type `N`.
  Sizes keep their types. Wrap each largest `nat` size expression in `N.of_nat` once when it
  enters a time bound. Use `%N` on time statements and an `N`-valued size for `bigO`.
- Time proofs use `time_step`, `time_cbn` and `time_pose` (Argent), then `lia` or `nia`.
  Plain `cbn` and `simpl` unfold binary arithmetic on numerals. Read the archival design's time
  statements through `docs/design/rules.md` section 8.

## 5. When stuck
- 20 minutes on one lemma with no progress: leave it Admitted, keep its statement,
  add (* OPEN: what is missing *), go to the next item.
- A PLAN lemma that is false or useless: prove the contract it serves another way,
  leave the lemma Admitted with (* PLAN-DEFECT: reason, counterexample if any *).
- A LAWS or TIME statement that is false: stop work on that component, leave it
  Admitted with (* SIG-QUESTION: reason *), and move to the next component.
- A CONVERT proof: port the old proof named in the tag; renaming and unfolding are allowed.
- Never weaken a statement, add a hypothesis to a Sig lemma, or edit outside your paths.
